    One day in Medianville

    Medianville is a simulated American city of about 100,000 people, built to match the country as a whole. This is one day there, Wednesday, March 3, 2027: about 200,000 transactions. Paychecks, bus fares, autopays, declined cards, hours of unpaid caregiving.

    Each one is a job to be done: something a person was trying to get done. Seen together, they show how jobs collide (a bill that drafts at 2 a.m. declines a card at 5 p.m.) and the systems underneath that decide who they work for. The goal is to find small, practical fixes for daily life at the margins.

    Households, wages, prices and payment habits are calibrated to federal data from the Census, BLS and the Federal Reserve, and rates such as fees, loans, shutoffs and caregiving hours are set from 42 public datasets. A simulator settles every dollar and every minute. Federal programs keep their real names. Every person, business and street is invented.

    It started with Jason James asking which American town is most like America. No real one is, so he set out to build one. Jason came up with the idea and directs the project. Claude, Anthropic’s AI model, wrote the simulator, generated the days, hand-wrote 300 of this day’s records (500 in the first version) and built this viewer with him.
