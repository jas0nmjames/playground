# Seeing the Margins: What a Representative American Day Reveals

Nov 16, 2026 · @Jason James

## Executive summary

No real city stands in for the United States, so the most defensible way to study an ordinary American day is a synthetic city built from national data and checked against real places. The larger finding is that the margins are wide and stable. Roughly a third of adults cannot cover a $400 surprise with cash, and payment habits are splitting by age, income and place. Meanwhile the public data used to see these problems is shrinking.

**Key findings**

1. **Representativeness depends on what you measure.** Different studies crown New Haven, Indianapolis or Nashville. The places closest to the national mix are mid-size metros of 1 to 2 million people; small towns are the least representative.
2. **America is mostly suburban and service-based.** 55% of Americans live in suburban and small-metro counties. Health care and state and local government are the two largest employers.
3. **Fragility is the norm, not the edge.** 63% of adults would cover a $400 expense with cash or its equivalent, unchanged for four years. About 1 in 4 renters fell behind on rent in the past year, and 1 in 4 adults skipped medical care because of cost.
4. **Payments are diverging.** Cash is 14% of payments overall, but 21% in rural areas and 19% for people 55 and older.
5. **Government is part of nearly every day,** as transfers in, services delivered, payers behind health care and taxes out. Most transaction data captures only the first and last of these.
6. **The evidence base is thinning.** The food-security survey was discontinued, the main survey of month-to-month income churn was cut and is being redesigned, and the CFPB has lost about 40% of its staff.
7. **Needs move on four clocks:** the calendar, the life course, the generation and the community's life cycle. A single snapshot misses most of them.

**Recommendations in brief**

- Simulate nationally, verify locally, and calibrate against published benchmarks.
- Count failures, waits, fees and unpaid effort as transactions.
- Make every role of government visible in the data.
- Read each friction through paired left and right diagnoses, and favor projects both support.
- Archive public datasets now and record the vintage of every source.

## No single place is America

Every attempt to find the "most typical" American place picks a different winner, because each one measures different things.

| Study | What it measured | Most representative | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Jed Kolko, FiveThirtyEight (2016) | Age, education, race and ethnicity | New Haven, CT (93.2), then Tampa (91.6) and Hartford (90.2) | [Hartford Business Journal](https://hartfordbusiness.com/article/hartford-3rd-most-typical-city-in-america/) |
| WalletHub (2016) | 26 metrics across 379 metros, including income, household makeup and housing tenure | Indianapolis (93.50), then Cincinnati and Oklahoma City | [WalletHub](https://wallethub.com/edu/metro-areas-that-most-and-least-resemble-the-us/6109); [Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/nashville/commercial-real-estate/metro-nashville-no-7-in-resembling-us-as-a-whole-61652) |
| WalletHub (2014) | Demographics of 366 metros | Nashville overall; Denver on race, Tulsa on age, Detroit on income | [Durango Herald](https://dh.durangoherald.com/?p=404677) |
| Karl Sluis (2017) | Eight ACS measures near the national median | Lynchburg, VA | [FlowingData](https://flowingdata.com/2017/03/14/most-typical-city-in-america/) |
| Restaurant and retail test markets | Practical fit for product launches | Columbus, OH | [Nation's Restaurant News](https://www.nrn.com/archive/why-chains-pick-columbus-ohio-test-market-target) |

Two patterns hold across studies. First, Kolko found that the most representative places tend to be larger metros but not the largest ones; similarity peaks for metros of 1 to 2 million people, and metros under 100,000 look least like the country ([Planetizen summary](https://planetizen.com/node/86037/normal-america-according-demographics)). Second, the places that most resemble 1950s America today are small metros and rural areas, so the common image of "real America" as a small town is decades out of date.

Even test-market cities are a compromise. Ohio State marketing faculty have questioned whether Columbus still mirrors US demographics as well as it once did ([Ohio State](https://fst.osu.edu/news/some-things-columbus-still-best-test-market)).

**Implication.** Any single real city misses rural life, which is where service gaps are widest, and none of these rankings measured industry mix. A synthetic city sampled from national microdata avoids both problems, and the rankings become a check: Medianville should score at least as well as the best real metro on each study's measures.

## The shape of the country

Most Americans live in suburbs and smaller metros and work in services, especially care and public services.

**Where people live.** By Pew Research Center's county classification, about 31% of Americans live in urban core counties of large metros, 55% in suburban and small-metro counties, and 14% in rural counties ([Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/2018/05/22/demographic-and-economic-trends-in-urban-suburban-and-rural-communities/)). Suburbs have grown fastest since 2000, and rural counties are older on average.

**What people do for work.** In the 2025 BLS employment projections base year, the largest sectors are:

| Sector | Share of US employment, 2025 |
| --- | --- |
| Health care and social assistance | 13.7% |
| State and local government | 12.1% |
| Retail trade | 9.1% |
| Accommodation and food services | 8.3% |
| Manufacturing | 7.4% |
| Professional, scientific and technical services | 6.3% |

Source: [BLS Employment Projections, employment by major industry sector](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/employment-by-major-industry-sector.htm). Self-employment adds another 5.8%.

**Implication.** A representative day is dominated by care work, public services, retail and food service, often on shifts and at modest wages. Many of the margin problems worth solving sit where those workers are also the customers: getting to a shift, being paid on time, and managing a household around irregular hours.

## Fragility at the margins

Financial fragility is a large, stable condition, not a small edge case. More than a third of adults could not cover a $400 surprise with cash in any year of the last decade.

&#91;embedded content: Federal Reserve SHED, 2015–2025, as compiled by Financer.com from the Fed's data; 2021 and 2025 values confirmed in the Fed's 2025 report\]

The Federal Reserve's 2025 Survey of Household Economics and Decisionmaking (SHED) adds detail ([Fed executive summary](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2026-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2025-executive-summary.htm)):

- 73% of adults were doing at least okay financially, but low-income, young and Black adults saw meaningful declines.
- 23% of renters were behind on rent at some point in the past year, 6 points higher than in 2021.
- 26% of adults skipped medical care because of cost.
- 20% of adults experienced fraud or scams. Among adults earning under $50,000, the median loss was $400.
- 42% said finding or keeping a job was a concern, up from 37% in 2024.

The ALICE measure from United For ALICE counts households that work but earn too little to cover a local survival budget. In Georgia, 45% of households were below the ALICE threshold in 2023, combining those in poverty with those above it but unable to afford basics ([2025 ALICE Report, Georgia](https://dev.uwcsra.web8.m3agency.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/2025-ALICE-Report-Georgia-print-version.pdf)). Statewide, the occupations with the highest shares of workers below the threshold include cooks, cashiers, stockers and fast-food workers ([United Way of Greater Atlanta](https://unitedwayatlanta.org/34-of-georgians-are-alice-asset-limited-income-constrained-and-employed/)).

**Implication.** The margin is not a minority problem. A small fee, a late deposit or a lost day of pay hits a large share of households at once. Small fixes to those moments can have broad reach.

## How people pay, and how that is splitting

Americans made about 47 payments a month in 2025, and cards dominate. But cash use now differs sharply by age, income and place, so one payment design does not fit everyone.

The Federal Reserve's 2026 Diary of Consumer Payment Choice, covering 2025, found ([Federal Reserve Financial Services](https://www.frbservices.org/binaries/content/assets/crsocms/news/research/2026-diary-consumer-payments-choice.pdf)):

- **Mix:** about 16 credit card, 15 debit card and 6.5 cash payments per person per month. Cash was 14% of all payments.
- **Small purchases:** five of six cash payments were under $25.
- **Persistence:** 76% carried cash, averaging $69, and about 90% plan to keep using it.

| Group | Cash payments per month | Cash share of payments |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Age 55 and older | 10 | 19% |
| Age 25 to 54 | 5 | 10% |
| Under 25 | 2 | 6% |
| Rural | 9 | 21% |
| Suburban | 6 | 13% |
| Urban | 6 | 12% |

Rural consumers also made fewer payments overall: 42 a month, against 53 in urban areas. The same report notes that in 2016, cash was about a third of payments regardless of age, and by 2025 the gaps by age and income had widened.

**Implication.** Moves toward cashless stores, app-only parking, online-only benefits and card-only transit put the burden on older, rural and lower-income residents first. Any project that changes how people pay should be tested against these groups.

## Government in daily life

Government shapes a typical day in four roles, but card and bank data capture only two of them: money in and taxes out. A view of everyday needs that leaves out free and subsidized services, or the insurers and programs paying behind the counter, will misjudge where people struggle.

| Role | Examples | Visible in card or bank data? | How Medianville records it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Transfers in | Social Security, SSI, SNAP, tax refunds, unemployment, VA benefits | Yes, as deposits or EBT loads | Benefit records from a government party to a household, on real payment schedules |
| Services delivered | Public school days, library visits, transit rides, public clinics, parks, senior meals | Rarely; the price to the user is often zero | Service-encounter records with the user's cost, the payer and an estimated public cost |
| Payers behind a purchase | Medicare, Medicaid, private insurers, school districts, employers | Only the user's copay | Split amounts: what the user paid and what a third party paid |
| Taxes out | Sales tax, payroll withholding, property tax | Partly | Fields on each purchase and payroll record |

The Congressional Budget Office measures these flows across the income distribution. Its September 2026 report covers 2023, the latest year with full tax data, and traces income before and after means-tested transfers and federal taxes since 1979 ([CBO, The Distribution of Household Income, 2023](https://www.cbo.gov/system/files/2026-09/62761-Household-Income-2023.pdf)). Those results are the natural check on whether Medianville's transfers and taxes are the right size for each income group.

Infrastructure used by everyone, such as roads, water and police, does not need a record per person. It belongs in a context file of daily public costs and funding sources, so analysts can see the full public budget behind the day.

**Implication.** Many margin problems sit at the seams between roles: a benefit that arrives two days after rent is due, a copay owed before an insurer pays, a free service that is hard to reach. Recording all four roles makes those seams visible.

## A thinning evidence base

Several of the public measures best suited to seeing the margins were cut, paused or redesigned in 2025 and 2026. That makes careful synthetic data and field observation more useful, and it makes archiving existing data urgent.

| Change | What it measured | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CPS Food Security Supplement discontinued in 2025 | Household and child food insecurity, tracked for about 30 years | [AEI](https://cosm.aei.org/the-end-of-the-food-security-supplement-in-the-current-population-survey-next-steps/); [InsuranceNewsNet](https://insurancenewsnet.com/oarticle/new-census-data-underscore-importance-of-food-assistance-and-health-coverage-access) |
| Survey of Income and Program Participation: FY2026 request cut about $40 million; redesign under field test | Month-to-month changes in income, jobs and benefits | [The Census Project, June 2025](https://thecensusproject.org/2025/06/30/department-of-commerce-releases-fiscal-year-2026-census-budget-details/); [August 2026](https://thecensusproject.org/2026/08/10/census-bureau-plans-new-sipp-method-field-test/) |
| 2026 Government Units Survey canceled | Structure of local governments | [PolicyRisk](https://policyrisk.com/behind-the-curtain/federal-register/2025-06303) |
| A federal survey of farmworkers canceled amid statistical staff losses | Farm labor conditions | [Yahoo News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/scores-government-statisticians-gone-leaving-221238525.html) |
| CFPB staff fell from 1,750 to 1,072 by August 1, 2026 | Consumer finance supervision and complaints | [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/cfpb-halts-plan-to-force-regional-workers-to-move-to-d-c) |
| CFPB closed about 40% of pending investigations and started one new public enforcement action in the year to June 30, 2026 | Enforcement against consumer-finance harms | [National Mortgage Professional](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/cfpb-closes-40-investigations-keeps-mortgage-enforcement-focus) |

Views differ on these changes. Supporters argue some surveys were obsolete or duplicative and that the CFPB overreached. Critics argue the cuts remove the country's main view of hardship and consumer harm. Either way, the practical effect for this project is the same: fewer fresh public numbers on the margins ([Federal News Network](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/big-data/2025/12/bedrock-federal-data-sets-are-disappearing-as-statistical-agencies-face-upheaval)).

**Implication.** Download and version the datasets this project depends on now, including ACS microdata, historical food-security files and the CFPB complaint database. Record the vintage of every number. Treat field visits as a primary source, not a garnish.

## Change over time

Needs move on four clocks at once, so a single snapshot of one day shows only part of the picture.

| Clock | What moves | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calendar | Paydays, benefit dates, the 1st of the month, tax-refund season, school year, storms | Benefit and payroll schedules; daily payment diaries |
| Life course | Births, school, work, partnering, illness, caregiving, retirement, widowhood | Long-running family panels such as PSID and the Health and Retirement Study |
| Generation | The same job done differently by different cohorts | Cash use was similar across ages in 2016 but diverged by 2025 ([Fed payment diary](https://www.frbservices.org/binaries/content/assets/crsocms/news/research/2026-diary-consumer-payments-choice.pdf)) |
| Community | Districts are built, mature, age, decline or renew | Suburbs are aging fastest: their 65-and-older population grew 39% from 2000, against 26% in urban and 22% in rural counties ([Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2018/05/22/key-findings-about-american-life-in-urban-suburban-and-rural-areas/)) |

CBO's Demographic Outlook for 2026 to 2056 gives the population projections needed to build future versions of the city ([CBO publications list](https://www.cbo.gov/system/files/2026-04/62155-work-in-progress.pdf)). The Economic Innovation Group's Distressed Communities Index sorts zip codes into five tiers from prosperous to distressed using seven indicators, including adults not working, housing vacancy and business growth, which makes it a ready yardstick for how places rise and fall ([Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/civic-news/distressed-communities-index-eig-map-delawaredistressed-communities-index-eig-map-delaware/)).

**Separate age, cohort and era.** A behavior that differs by age may come with a life stage and recur in each generation, belong to one cohort and age with it, or come from the era and change everyone. Projects built on the wrong reading will age badly.

**Implication.** Outer-ring suburbs built for young families are becoming places of older residents who drive, live alone and will need care, while their roads and pipes come due for replacement. That shift is slow, predictable and under-designed for.

## Reading the margins through many lenses

The same friction looks different depending on who is describing it, and the strongest projects hold up under more than one diagnosis.

**Advocacy lenses** each add something a demographic sample misses:

- **Labor (AFL-CIO and member unions):** shift timing, schedule volatility, pay cadence and safety. Union members are a small share of private-sector workers, 5.9% in 2025 ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/union2.t03.htm)), so most workers face these issues without a contract.
- **Farmworker advocates:** seasonal and guest-worker households, employer housing, heat and cash wages.
- **Aging (AARP):** unpaid caregiving, fixed-income payment dates, fraud exposure and digital-access limits.
- **Land use (Strong Towns):** the cost of distance and whether a place's development pattern pays for its own infrastructure.
- **Consumer finance (CFPB, consumer law groups):** fees, credit, debt collection and complaints.
- **Working households (United For ALICE):** families who earn above poverty but below a survival budget.
- **Workers' and consumers' stories (A More Perfect Union):** a check that the frictions in the model match what people describe.

**Left-right pairs.** For each topic, read one source from each side. Examples: Economic Policy Institute and American Compass on wages; Urban Institute and the AEI Housing Center on housing; Roosevelt Institute and Cato or Mercatus on regulation. In Georgia, the Georgia Budget and Policy Institute is generally described as liberal-leaning and the Georgia Public Policy Foundation as conservative-leaning ([The Citizen](https://thecitizen.com/2025/03/18/the-georgia-budget-policy-institute/)); GPPF describes itself as a free-market think tank ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Public_Policy_Foundation)), and GBPI as nonpartisan.

**Academic grounding.** NBER research on household transaction data, drawn from banks, fintech apps and payment processors, is the closest published analog to this project's method ([Baker and Kueng, NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w29027)). NBER working papers are not peer reviewed, so check for published versions.

**Implication.** For each top friction, write one diagnosis from each side. A home-care aide stuck at a low income might be described by one side as facing a benefits cliff and by the other as facing a licensing barrier. Fixes that address both diagnoses are more likely to work and easier to win support for.

## Recommendations

Build the city from national data, measure what people lose as well as what they spend, and choose projects that survive several lenses.

1. **Simulate nationally, verify locally.** Sample households from ACS microdata weighted to the whole country, give districts the national urban-suburban-rural split, and use nearby real places only for texture and field checks.
2. **Calibrate against published benchmarks.** At minimum: about 47 payments per person per month and 14% cash (Fed payment diary); 63% able to cover $400 with cash (SHED); spending shares from the BLS Consumer Expenditure Survey; income after transfers and taxes by group (CBO); and the Kolko and WalletHub similarity scores.
3. **Count failure, time and effort as transactions.** Declines, repeat trips, waits, fees and unpaid care are where the margin shows. Link each retry to its first attempt.
4. **Make all four roles of government visible:** transfers in, services delivered, payers behind a purchase, and taxes out, with an estimated public cost for free services.
5. **Simulate more than one day.** At least a weekday, a Saturday, the 1st of the month, a tax-refund day and a storm day, then move households forward through life events and build past and future versions of the city.
6. **Tag every record by lens** (labor, aging, rural, consumer finance, disability, land use and others) so problems can be sorted by who would recognize them.
7. **Pair diagnoses from left and right** for every top friction, and favor projects both support.
8. **Rank projects by reach and severity:** how often a friction occurs, its cost in money and time, and how concentrated it is among fragile households.
9. **Archive and version the data now,** given cuts to federal surveys, and note the vintage of every figure.
10. **Keep it honest.** Synthetic data generates hypotheses; it is not evidence. Confirm each candidate project with field observation and, where possible, conversations with the people it would affect. Observe public spaces only and record no one without consent.

## Candidate project hypotheses

These are starting hypotheses drawn from the research so far, to be tested against the simulated day and field visits, not conclusions.

| Hypothesis | Who it hits most | Why it looks promising | How to test | Lenses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Money arrives after it is due: benefit and pay dates out of sync with rent and bills cause late fees and overdrafts | Renters, benefit recipients, hourly workers | 23% of renters were behind on rent in the past year (SHED) | Count fees and late payments within a few days of pay or benefit dates | Consumer finance, labor, housing |
| Document readiness: trips to license offices and benefit renewals fail for a missing paper | Older adults, people with limited English, rural residents | Time costs fall on those with the least slack | Repeat-trip and missing-document tags; DDS and benefits-office visits | Aging, rural, disability, digital access |
| Small cash payments are losing places to happen | Rural, older and lower-income residents | Rural residents use cash for 21% of payments; five of six cash payments are under $25 (Fed diary) | Count declined or diverted cash payments at card-only sites | Rural, aging, consumer finance |
| Scam losses hit those least able to absorb them | Older and lower-income adults | Median fraud loss of $400 for adults under $50,000 income (SHED) | Scam-attempt tags by age and income | Aging, consumer finance |
| Skipped care cascades into larger costs | Uninsured and underinsured adults, rural residents | 26% skipped medical care because of cost (SHED) | Link skipped visits to later emergency visits in multi-day runs | Health cost, rural |
| Benefits cliffs stall raises | Low-wage workers with children | Paired left and right diagnoses both point at work disincentives | Model net income at small raises using Atlanta Fed cliff tools | Labor, family care, public finance |
| Caregiving time crowds out paid work | Unpaid family caregivers who also work | Unpaid time is invisible in spending data | Caregiver minutes against lost shift hours | Aging, family care, labor |
| Distance is a hidden tax in car-dependent districts | Outer-suburb and rural households without a reliable car | Strong Towns' land-use lens; rural thinning of services | Travel miles and failed trips by district and vehicle access | Land use, rural, digital access |

## Limitations

- **Rankings are dated.** The Kolko and WalletHub representativeness studies use data from 2014 to 2016 and should be recomputed on current ACS data.
- **Some figures are secondary.** The 2015 to 2024 values in the SHED chart come from a compilation of Fed data; the 2021 and 2025 values were confirmed in the Fed's report. Some sources here were read through news summaries rather than the original study, as linked.
- **Georgia is not the nation.** ALICE figures cited are for Georgia; national ALICE rates should be pulled before calibrating Medianville.
- **Political labels are approximate.** Many think tanks describe themselves as nonpartisan.
- **Synthetic data reflects its assumptions.** It can suggest where to look, but only observation and the people affected can confirm a problem.
